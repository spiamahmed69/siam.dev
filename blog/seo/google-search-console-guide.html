Google Search Console Setup Guide

By SP Siam Ahmed · Published 26 November 2026 · Updated 26 November 2026

Google Search Console is Google's free tool that shows you how your site appears in search results: which pages are indexed, which queries bring visitors, and what is broken. If you have a website, setting it up is one of the first things you should do — it costs nothing and it tells you the truth about your site.

What Is Google Search Console?

Search Console is the official channel between your website and Google Search. It does not improve your rankings by itself — no tool does — but it shows you what Google sees: crawl errors, indexing problems, mobile usability issues, and the actual search queries that lead people to your pages. Without it, you are guessing. With it, you are reading the report.

It is free, it works for any site, and it is provided by Google itself at Google Search Console.

Add Your Property

Sign in with a Google account and click Add property. Choose the URL prefix option and enter your exact site address — for example https://spiamahmed69.github.io/siam.dev/. The URL prefix must match your site exactly, including https:// and any trailing slash. The alternative, Domain property, covers all subdomains but requires DNS verification.

Verify Ownership

Google needs proof that the site is yours. The simplest method for a static site is the HTML tag: Search Console gives you a <meta> tag to paste into the <head> of your homepage. Add it, deploy, and click Verify. Other methods include uploading an HTML file to your site root, or adding a DNS TXT record at your registrar. Any one method is enough — pick the one that fits your setup.

Verification is a one-time step. Once verified, the property stays yours, and you can add other people (for example a collaborator) with limited permissions.

Submit Your Sitemap

After verification, open Sitemaps in the left menu and submit your sitemap URL — usually https://yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml. A sitemap is a simple XML file listing your pages; it helps Google discover them, especially new ones. Your sitemap must list only pages that actually exist, and every URL in it must return a 200 response.

Then use URL Inspection to check your homepage: paste the URL, and Search Console will tell you whether Google can index it, when it was last crawled, and whether the mobile version renders correctly. If a page is not indexed, the inspection tool will usually say why — and it has a Request Indexing button for pages you have just fixed.

Reports Worth Reading

  • Performance: clicks, impressions, average position and the exact queries people searched. Sort by impressions to find pages that appear in search but are rarely clicked — those are title and description problems.
  • Pages (Indexing): which pages are indexed and which are not, with reasons. Fix the reasons, not the symptom.
  • Core Web Vitals: real-user speed data, grouped into Good, Needs Improvement and Poor. See Core Web Vitals Explained.
  • Mobile Usability: problems that affect phone visitors — text too small, clickable elements too close together, content wider than the screen.
  • Links: which sites link to you, and which of your own pages get the most internal links.

Common Issues

“Page is not indexed” usually means the page is new (give it time), blocked by robots.txt, marked noindex, or a duplicate of another page. “Crawled — currently not indexed” often means Google considers the page thin or duplicative — add real, unique content. “Discovered — currently not indexed” means Google knows the URL exists but has not crawled it yet; a sitemap and internal links help.

Check Google Search Status if something looks wrong across the board — occasionally the problem is on Google's side, not yours.

Conclusion

Search Console is free, official and honest: it shows you what Google sees, what people search for, and what is broken. Set it up the day your site launches, submit your sitemap, and check the reports once a week. Most SEO improvement is just fixing what the reports already told you.
