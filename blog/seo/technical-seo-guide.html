Website SEO

Technical SEO Checklist

By SP Siam Ahmed · Published 26 November 2026 · Updated 26 November 2026

Technical SEO is the part of search optimisation that has nothing to do with writing and everything to do with engineering: can search engines find your pages, understand them, and load them quickly on a phone? This checklist covers the essentials — run through it for every page you publish.

Titles and Meta Descriptions

  • Every page has a unique <title> — descriptive, under about 60 characters, with the most important words first.
  • Every page has a unique meta description — a real summary of the page, under about 160 characters. No duplicates, no keyword stuffing.
  • The title and the <h1> agree with each other; they do not have to be identical, but they should describe the same page.

Canonicals and URLs

  • Every page has a canonical URL pointing to itself — the one address you want Google to index.
  • URLs are clean and descriptive: /blog/seo/technical-seo-guide.html, not /page?id=47.
  • One page, one URL. Avoid publishing the same content at two addresses.

Sitemap and Robots.txt

  • A sitemap.xml lists every real, indexable page — and nothing else. No 404s, no redirects, no duplicates.
  • A robots.txt allows crawling and points to the sitemap. Check it does not accidentally block your pages.
  • Submit the sitemap in Google Search Console and check that Google reads it without errors.

Structure and Headings

  • Semantic HTML throughout: <header>, <nav>, <main>, <article>, <footer>. See HTML Semantic Elements Explained.
  • One <h1> per page, headings in order (H1, then H2, then H3), never skipped levels for styling.
  • Breadcrumbs on subpages, with matching BreadcrumbList structured data.

Structured Data

  • Valid JSON-LD on every page where it applies: Person and WebSite on the homepage, Article on blog posts, BreadcrumbList on subpages.
  • Only include information that is genuinely true. No fake reviews, ratings or business details.
  • Test with the Google Rich Results Test before publishing.

Speed and Mobile

  • Mobile-first design: the site must work perfectly on a phone, because Google indexes the mobile version first.
  • Core Web Vitals in the green: LCP under 2.5 seconds, INP under 200 ms, CLS under 0.1. See Core Web Vitals Explained.
  • HTTPS everywhere, with no mixed content. Test with PageSpeed Insights.

Links and Images

  • Every internal link points to a page that exists. Check regularly — broken links waste crawl budget and trust.
  • Every image has descriptive alt text, real dimensions, and a modern format where possible. See Image Optimization for Faster Websites.
  • External links use target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" and point to real, relevant pages.

Conclusion

Technical SEO is not mysterious: unique titles, clean URLs, a real sitemap, semantic HTML, valid structured data, fast loading and a mobile-friendly design. Do those things on every page, verify them with Search Console, PageSpeed Insights and the Rich Results Test, and your site will be easy for Google to find, understand and recommend. The Google Search Essentials and the SEO Starter Guide are the official references behind every item on this list.
